# Satark's detector can be validated only against simulation

No public dataset links call, app-install and payment events for Indian coached UPI scams, so Satark's 16-feature logistic regression with per-cohort conformal thresholds can be trained and evaluated only on simulated sequences. Real inputs are limited to published aggregates: UPI volumes, ticket sizes, fraud counts and losses, and a few amount-band shares. Even those were mostly read through news and search summaries rather than primary documents, because the research sandbox blocked NPCI, RBI, Sansad, Kaggle, Hugging Face and arXiv. The decisive quantitative finding is that at the reported base rate of about **1 in 1.47 lakh transactions (6.8e-6)**, a detector with 80% recall and a 0.1% false-alarm rate has a precision of only about **0.54%**, roughly one true alert per 185 alerts. A conformal alpha of 0.001 is a false-alarm budget and not a precision target. The recommended build is therefore a labelled simulator (SatarkSim) in which every parameter carries a tag of real, secondhand or assumed. It has separate seeds for train, calibration, validation and test, a perturbed test generator, sensitivity sweeps and a simple-rule baseline. Results are reported as simulated ranges, never as expected field performance. Real legitimate-traffic shadow mode is the only way to check the false-alarm guarantee honestly.

## Three tiers of inputs: what is real, secondhand or assumed

The team asked for no dummy data, and the honest answer is that very little of what the simulator needs exists as verified real data. The notes support a three-label scheme. "Real" means the source was actually read in the sandbox. "Secondhand" means the figure comes from a press outlet or search summary relaying an official or industry document that was not opened. "Assumed" means a design choice with no data behind it. The lines between tiers matter more than the totals.

Only a handful of items reached the **real, directly read** tier, and none of them are UPI behaviour data. The Feedzai Bank Account Fraud (BAF) datasheet and paper PDFs were downloaded and read ([datasheet](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/feedzai/bank-account-fraud/main/documents/datasheet.pdf), [paper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/feedzai/bank-account-fraud/main/documents/BAF_paper.pdf)). They show that BAF is a bank account-opening dataset with 1.1% prevalence, generated by CTGAN from an anonymised real dataset, so it is synthetic-from-real and not a payments dataset. The 5,574-row UCI SMS Spam Collection was pulled through a GitHub mirror ([mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/justmarkham/pycon-2016-tutorial/master/data/sms.tsv)), but it is English-language SMS text and irrelevant to the on-device features. The ICFD-31k dataset card was read and states explicitly that its Indian fraud-call conversations are synthetic ([dataset card](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SPELLAILab/ICFD-31k/main/DATASET_CARD.md)), with licence terms limiting use to defensive research ([licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SPELLAILab/ICFD-31k/main/LICENSE-DATA.md)). The PhonePe Pulse repository README was also read: it offers quarterly state- and district-level aggregates under CDLA-Permissive-2.0, with no age bands, hour-of-day or amount distribution ([PhonePe Pulse](https://github.com/PhonePe/pulse)). Finally, the Android platform facts that the notes quote from fetched pages are reasonably firm: without READ_CALL_LOG the number is empty ([Android 9 changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/pie/android-9.0-changes-all)), and manifest receivers cannot receive PACKAGE_ADDED on API 26+ ([broadcast exceptions](https://developer.android.com/guide/components/broadcast-exceptions)). Of the remote-access watch-list, only RustDesk (com.carriez.flutter_hbb, [F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/de/packages/com.carriez.flutter_hbb/)) and AirDroid Remote Support (com.sand.airsos, [APKMirror](https://www.apkmirror.com/?p=11671934), via a search result) were confirmed. Other package IDs are recalled from memory and must be checked on Play before shipping.

The **secondhand** tier holds every national statistic. UPI ran at 24.07 billion transactions and Rs 29.37 lakh crore in September 2026 ([StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/upi-transactions-september-2026/)), and FY25 volume was 185.8 billion transactions worth Rs 261 lakh crore per the RBI annual report as relayed by press ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/05/223-upi-84-india-fy25-retail-payment-volume-rbi/)). The Finance Ministry's Lok Sabha reply of 15 December 2025 gave UPI fraud of 13.42 lakh incidents and Rs 1,087 crore in FY2023-24, 12.64 lakh and Rs 981 crore in FY2024-25, and 10.64 lakh and Rs 805 crore for FY2025-26 through November ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/upi-frauds-worth-rs-805-crore-witnessed-this-fiscal-so-far-minister--20251215175703)); the Sansad PDF itself was never opened. The Worldline report puts calendar-2025 P2M average ticket at Rs 592 and overall average ticket at Rs 1,314 ([Worldline](https://worldline.com/en-in/home/top-navigation/media-relations/press-releases/worldline-reports-that-india-digital-payments-cross-new-milestones-as-upi-reaches-228-billion-transactions-in-2025)), with H1 2025 P2P and P2M counts and values in the H1 2025 report ([Worldline PDF](https://worldline.com/content/dam/worldline/local/en-in/documents/main-page11111/Worldline-India-Digital-Payment-Report-1H-2025-1.pdf)). The only published amount band is that about 86% of P2M and 59% of P2P transactions fall below Rs 500 in FY2025-26 ([Vision IAS](https://visionias.in/blog/current-affairs/upi-at-10-how-india-built-the-worlds-largest-real-time-payments-platform)), with the period and primary source unverified. The RBI April 2026 discussion paper, using NCRP data for all digital frauds and not UPI alone, says transactions above Rs 10,000 are about 45% of cases but 98.5% of value ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/rbi-proposes-1-hour-delay-for-transactions-above-10000-to-fight-digital-fraud)). Digital-arrest complaints rose from 39,925 (2022) to 1,23,672 (2024), with Rs 1,935.51 crore lost in 2024 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/3962-skype-ids-83668-whatsapp-accounts-used-for-digital-arrest-blocked-so-far-ministry-of-home-affairs-to-rajya-sabha)). Several of these series conflict across outlets (for example two RBI publications and two 2024 loss totals of Rs 22,848 crore versus Rs 11,333 crore), and the notes did not reconcile them. Under-reporting is also material: LocalCircles found 51% of victims filed no complaint ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/upi-transaction-fraud-india-survey-one-in-five-users-hit-localcircles-125062601141_1.html)).

The **assumed** tier is the largest and covers precisely what the detector's features depend on. No source gave new-payee rates, top-N payee concentration, per-user transaction counts by age, delay from call to app install, install-to-payment gaps, initial-call length, continuous video-session length, the share of sideloaded versus Play installs, the share of coached scams by playbook, or the frequency of legitimate look-alikes such as a family call during a payment or a bank-support call. The notes found no public dataset with call, install and payee fields at all, and add the caveat that this is absence of evidence from a sandbox that could not search Kaggle, Hugging Face or GitHub directly. The unknown-contact call, unknown video, install-during-call, remote-access-app, payee-newness, payee-added-after-call and sequence features all have to be simulated. The notes do not list all 16 features, so the report does not name the other nine.

## At the real base rate, precision collapses even at 0.1% false alarms

The base rate comes from dividing 12.64 lakh FY25 UPI fraud incidents by about 185.9 billion transactions, giving about 6.8e-6, or 1 in 1.47 lakh. The FY26 partial year gives about 1 in 1.5 lakh. That is the notes' own arithmetic. The FY25 volume used in the divisor is flagged in the notes as unverified background knowledge, though the RBI-reported 185.8 billion cited above is consistent with it. Two caveats apply. The ratio mixes a Finance Ministry fraud count with an RBI volume, and reporting bases may differ. Reported cases are also an undercount, so 6.8e-6 is a floor. The implied mean reported loss is about Rs 7,760, and it is an all-fraud-types mean, not a coached-scam one.

Bayes does the rest. With recall r, false-alarm rate f and prevalence pi, precision is r*pi / (r*pi + f*(1-pi)). At r = 0.8 and f = 0.001, precision is about **0.54%**. Per 147,000 sessions that means roughly 147 false alarms against about 0.8 true alarms. My extension of the same formula shows that f = 1e-4 would lift precision only to about 5%. To reach 50% precision, f would have to be near r*pi, about 5.4e-6, which implies a conformal alpha of roughly 1 in 185,000 and calibration sets in the hundreds of thousands. The practical consequence is that alerts must be positioned as a soft step-up or review signal, not an accusation, and every report must show precision at several base rates (1e-5, 1/147,000, 1e-4 and the training rate) with intervals propagated from recall and false-alarm uncertainty, alongside alerts per 100,000 sessions. Any evaluation set with 1% to 3.5% positives, such as BAF at 1.1% or IEEE-CIS at 3.5% ([FDB README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/amazon-science/fraud-dataset-benchmark/main/README.md)), is an enriched sample whose precision says nothing about deployment.

Class weighting does not threaten the conformal step. Weighting or prior correction shifts the logit by a roughly constant amount (about -11.9 for the real prior under balanced weighting), so ranking is unchanged, and a threshold computed on legitimate scores on the same scale gives the same alarm decisions. The notes cite the scikit-learn documentation for how `class_weight='balanced'` is computed ([scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.LogisticRegression.html)) and King and Zeng on rare-event bias ([JSS](https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v008i02)), but the shift itself is the notes' own derivation, to be confirmed by tests. The notes recommend plain weights over SMOTE, because synthetic rows deepen the simulator circularity.

## A calibrated simulator: parameters and their labels

SatarkSim should be two generators plus a sequence composer. The legitimate generator produces per-user UPI histories and sessions, and the scam generator injects playbook sequences into some users' sessions. Every parameter below is stored in a single file with a `provenance` field (real, secondhand or assumed), a source URL where one exists, and a range for sweeps. Where the notes provide a recommendation I take it; the unlabelled cells are the notes' own wording or my arithmetic on them, and values marked "design" are choices of mine for the build plan that no source supports.

The playbooks give the simulator its scam shapes. The digital-arrest chain runs from an unknown caller (courier, TRAI or telecom regulator) to fake police on a Skype or WhatsApp video call, followed by "verification" transfers ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/national/bengaluru-woman-loses-rs-3183-cr-in-months-long-digital-arrest-scam-involving-fake-cbi-officers-853424)). The remote-access KYC chain has a fake bank or telecom agent push AnyDesk, TeamViewer or QuickSupport installation ([Zee5](https://zee5.com/articles/two-arrested-for-cyber-fraud-through-fake-kyc-links-in-telangana)). The APK chain uses fake RTO e-challan files sent over SMS or WhatsApp ([Cyfirma](https://www.cyfirma.com/research/rto-challan-fraud-a-technical-report-on-apk-based-financial-and-identity-theft)). One structural caution comes from the notes' inference: digital-arrest payments are mostly RTGS or NEFT account transfers, so a UPI detector should treat them as large P2P-like transfers, and the share of UPI coached scams that look like them is unknown. The 2-hour long-session feature is consistent with reported durations but untested, since no source measured continuous call time. Also, Play-store remote-access apps and a sideloaded APK are indistinguishable by app identity alone, so install source (Play versus sideload, reliable only on API 30+) is the discriminating signal.

Android capture limits also shape what the simulator must emit. Call numbers are unavailable without READ_CALL_LOG, which Play restricts, and WhatsApp or Skype video calls generally do not appear as telephony OFFHOOK, so video sessions come from UsageStats and are lower bounds. These are the notes' inferences from memory and need verification against documentation. The proposed event envelope carries a per-signal status (OK, DEGRADED, UNAVAILABLE), so the simulator should randomly mark signals unavailable at an assumed rate, letting the model learn the difference between "no call" and "cannot see".

## Conformal calibration at alpha = 0.001 needs at least 999 legitimate scores per cohort

The method is split conformal with Mondrian cohort splitting on legitimate-only scores ([Angelopoulos and Bates](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07511), [Mondrian discussion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09335)). The threshold is the k-th smallest legitimate calibration score with k = ceil((n+1)(1-alpha)), computed in integer arithmetic as ((n+1)*999 + 999) // 1000. If k exceeds n, the threshold is infinite and no alarm is possible. That makes n = 999 a hard floor, where the threshold equals the maximum score and is very noisy, with 3,000 to 10,000 a practical target. These are the notes' derivations from standard theory, not retrieved quotes, and should be pinned by tests.

The guarantee is marginal. Conditional on one calibration set, the realised false-alarm rate is roughly Beta distributed. At n = 999 it has mean 0.001 but a 95th percentile near 0.003. At n = 10,000 the relative spread is about 32%. When a "no more than 0.1% with 95% confidence" claim is needed, the training-conditional variant of Bates and colleagues ([Annals of Statistics 2023](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-51/issue-1/Testing-for-outliers-with-conformal-p-values/10.1214/22-AOS2244.full)) or a higher-order statistic is required. For cohorts with too few calibration users, the order of preference is a hierarchical fallback (cohort threshold if n_cohort is at least a set minimum such as 2,000, else the pooled threshold), conservative inflation, then clustering of similar cohorts. Never silently use a cohort threshold with k > n. Each cohort's n_cal, k and method ("cohort", "pooled" or "inf") should be exported to the JSON for audit.

Age is used only to select the threshold, never as a feature, which keeps legitimate false-alarm rates near alpha per band and targets the false-positive-rate parity that BAF-style audits measure ([FairGBM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.07850)). The 1.5x ratio gate on worst-to-best band false-alarm rates has no cited origin and is a project policy. With a few thousand sessions per band the ratio is estimated from a handful of events, so the gate should use confidence intervals, for example the upper bound of the worst band against the pooled alpha. Recall parity is a separate descriptive metric.

Calibration is valid only if calibration users are disjoint from training users and exchangeable with deployment legitimate sessions. That is the exact weak point of a simulator: exchangeability with simulated legitimate sessions says nothing about real users. Shadow mode on real legitimate traffic needs only legitimate scores, so it is the one real-data check that is both feasible and informative.

## Build plan, acceptance checks and the 36 test vectors

The steps below are the notes' recommendations arranged in build order. Thresholds in the acceptance checks come from the notes' derivations except where marked design.

| Step | Work | Acceptance check |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Parameter registry | One versioned file with value, range, tag (real / secondhand / assumed) and source URL per parameter; seeds for simulator, split and model. | Every parameter has a tag; no parameter lacks a source or an "assumed" label; CI fails on an untagged entry. |
| 2. Legitimate generator | Per-user histories (mixture amount model with round-number snap, negative-binomial counts, Zipf payees, assumed new-payee rates); hard negatives (family call to known payee, own-bank support call, IT session) labelled assumed. | Synthetic marginals reproduce 86% / 59% below Rs 500 and means near Rs 592 and Rs 2,640 within a stated tolerance (design: 2 percentage points on shares, 10% on means). |
| 3. Scam generator | Digital-arrest, remote-access KYC and APK playbooks with heavy-tailed amounts, bimodal durations and wide sweeps for unmeasured gaps; Android signal-unavailable noise. | Each playbook produces the ordered call, install, new-payee, pay sequence with its documented structure; unmeasured gaps are swept, not fixed. |
| 4. Feature pipeline | Pure functions of past events only: log amount ratio against the prior-window median (excluding the current transaction), robust z with 1.4826 scaling and a MAD=0 floor, cold-start blending, a few pre-specified interactions, the ordered-sequence boolean with stated gap bounds. | No look-ahead (test shuffling future events changes nothing); cold-start tests at n = 0, 1, 19, 20, 21; MAD=0 branch tested. |
| 5. Split and train | Split by user into train, calibration and validation (the notes suggest 50/25/25), stratified by scam presence and age band; scams never in calibration; separate seeds; weighted logistic regression, no SMOTE. | No user appears in two splits; every cohort has calibration users; a rule baseline and a z-score-only baseline are trained for comparison. |
| 6. Calibrate | Per-cohort split-conformal thresholds on legitimate logits with the integer k rule and hierarchical fallback. | n_cal, k and method exported per cohort; no cohort has k > n unless marked "inf"; tied-score test passes with strict ">" . |
| 7. Evaluate | Recall and false-alarm rate with Wilson or Clopper-Pearson intervals, cluster bootstrap by user, precision at several base rates, alerts per 100,000 sessions, per-band fairness with intervals. | Zero-alarm validation reports use the rule of three (at least 3,000 legitimate validation sessions to bound below 0.1%); precision is never reported at the training prevalence alone. |
| 8. Stress and circularity checks | Untouched final test seed; a perturbed test generator (different amount multipliers, new behaviours, shifted legitimate behaviour); sweeps over prevalence, amount multiplier, legitimate variability, MAD=0 rate and cold-start share. | Recall reported as a range across sweeps; false-alarm rate shown under shifted legitimate behaviour to show where the guarantee breaks; every table labelled "simulated". |
| 9. Export and Kotlin parity | JSON with float64 repr strings, fixed feature order, scaler mean and scale, coefficients, intercept, thresholds on the logit scale, clip bounds, MAD constant, library versions; sequential Double evaluation in Kotlin; decide on the logit, not the probability. | Features and logits match to 1e-9; alarm booleans match exactly except explicitly asserted near-ties; Locale.ROOT parsing; no Float. |
| 10. Real-data shadow mode | On real legitimate sessions only, compute scores and alarm rate against the conformal threshold. | Observed alarm rate is within the interval expected from the calibration size; this is the only step that can turn a simulated claim into a real one. |

For the 36 test vectors, the notes recommend 200 to 1,000 golden vectors and do not specify 36, so the allocation is my design, not a sourced figure. I suggest 36 as a fixed, hand-auditable core set generated by the Python script with a fixed seed, in six groups of six: ordinary legitimate sessions across cohorts; cold start (n = 0, 1, 19, 20, 21 history lengths plus one empty history); MAD=0 and clipping extremes (including |z| in the hundreds and very large amounts); conformal threshold edges (exact tie, just above and below the threshold, n at 998 and 999 cohorts giving "inf" and maximum thresholds); scam sequences in order, out of order and interleaved, with gap bounds inclusive at both ends; and signal-unavailable cases. Each vector holds raw inputs, intermediate features, the logit, the probability and the alarm. A larger bulk set of random vectors should run alongside it, since 36 cases cannot catch rare numerical drift. Vectors that come from simulated data must carry the "simulated" label in their file header.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The data does not exist to prove that Satark catches coached scams. What can be built honestly is a mechanically correct detector, with Kotlin parity to 1e-9, a conformal calibration whose false-alarm guarantee is well defined, and a simulator whose every assumption is visible. The base-rate arithmetic shows that even a perfectly working guarantee yields mostly false alerts at deployment, so the product claim must be "a small fraction of sessions flagged for a gentle pause", never "most alerts are scams".

Two routes could turn parts of this into real evidence. The first is shadow-mode legitimate scoring, which needs only the legitimate side and validates the false-alarm rate. The second is a data-sharing agreement with a bank, NPCI, I4C or telcos, or a labelled opt-in pilot, which the notes identify as the only realistic source of real coached-scam sequences. Before either, the highest-value low-cost work is verification: opening the Sansad answer AU2481, Rajya Sabha question 267 and the RBI annual report directly, and checking every watch-list package ID on Play, so that "secondhand" tags can be upgraded to "real" in the registry.
