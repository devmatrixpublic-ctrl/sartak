4. Why alpha' is 0.08% and not 0.1%, and what precision looks like

Conformal calibration at alpha = 0.001 makes the expected false-alarm rate 0.1%, but one calibration draw lands above or below it. Cross-validation (detector/experiments/calibration_margin_cv.md) measured calibration at alpha = 0.001 giving 0.107% false alarms, just over the target, in agreement with the earlier validation figure of 0.111%. Following a rule fixed before the run (largest alpha' with cross-validated false alarms <= 0.09%), alpha' = 0.0008 was chosen. Two other options were tried and not adopted: thresholds with a 90% confidence statement (detector/experiments/conformal_confidence.md: false alarms 0.040%, recall 92.6%) and session-total features (detector/experiments/session_total_features.md: no gain).

False alarms of 0.07% are rates among legitimate sessions in the simulator. At a real fraud rate of about 6.8e-06 per transaction (a rough, second-hand prior, order of magnitude only), with recall 93.1% and false alarms 0.071%, precision is about 0.89%, roughly one true alert per 113 alerts. A 0.1% false-alarm target is not a precision target.

10. Open items

  • Levels: the detector emits NONE or HARD. NUDGE and TRUSTED_CONTACT (in contracts/api.yaml) are not produced.
  • Baseline (tested, result is uncomfortable): the rolling last-50 median/MAD with an n/(n+20) blend toward a population prior is a design choice of the port. detector/experiments/baseline_cold_start.md compares a mature personal history with a brand-new install (population prior for everyone), each recalibrated: recall 93.1% vs 94.8% and false alarms 0.071% vs 0.111%, with overlapping intervals. SatarkSim therefore shows no benefit from personalised baselines, and with this data it cannot show they are useless either: its scam amounts are far above any user's normal, so the amount baseline barely matters. Do not present "adaptive per-user thresholds" as a measured result; it remains a design hypothesis until real or better-simulated data tests it.
  • Session boundaries: undefined by the reference; the app decides (app/.../SessionPolicy.kt).
  • Rule false alarms: SatarkSim has no genuine sideloads or long unknown-number video calls, so the rules' 0% false-alarm rate is untested against real behaviour.
  • Unknown video origin: the app infers it (VideoOrigin); the platform cannot say who is on a video call.
  • Tag: detector-freeze is not set; set it only after the run on physical phones and sign-off.

5. What this does and does not show

  • It shows the recall and false-alarm figures are not an accident of one random draw: across independent draws they move by the amounts in section 1.
  • It does not show real-world accuracy. Both the training data and this test come from the same simulator and share its assumptions.
  • Section 4 is the honest list of where the detector fails; each cause is a known limitation, not a surprise.
  • The sealed held-out run is still to do, and can only be done once. It is blocked on purpose until the detector-freeze tag exists.

Split-payment evasion and the split guard (simulated data)

Split-payment evasion: before and after a split guard (Person B)

Simulated data. Validation split of SatarkSim (578 scam sessions), detector exactly as shipped, thresholds not re-tuned (except the one labelled re-calibrated row). Not the sealed held-out set and not real fraud.

Attacks: each payment in a scam is split into k equal pieces to the same payee, 30 s apart (k = 1 is the unmodified data); and an *adaptive* attack that cuts each payment into the fewest pieces that are each no larger than the victim's own median payment (the amount the detector compares against), at most 50 pieces. Guard: each attempt is scored as the running total to that payee in the last 30 minutes. The guard is app-side pre-processing, not part of the frozen detector; adopting it needs a new freeze.

*Amount protected* is the share of the scam's total still unsent when the alarm fired (an alarm holds the attempt it fires on). It is computed from the real piece amounts, never from the guard's running totals.

4. Reading the results

  • Splitting did not evade the detector. Unattacked it catches 538/578; across every attack in section 1 the worst case is 538/578 caught with the guard off. 0 scams caught unattacked were missed after any split.
  • Why: each piece is another payment screen and another chance to alarm, and repeat payments to one payee add to the score, so more pieces give the detector more evidence, not less.
  • What splitting does cost the defender is timing. Interdicted before the first payment falls from 83.9% unattacked to 57.6% under the adaptive attack: the first small pieces go out before the alarm. The money at risk stays small (99.9% of the amount is still protected).
  • The guard does not fix that, and was not needed here. It leaves interdiction before the first payment unchanged (the first piece is scored as itself), and its only measurable effect is on scams whose own repeat payments add up. False alarms with the guard: 7/9887 against 7/9887 without.
  • So: do not ship the guard on this evidence. The honest statement for the deck is that this attack shape was tested and does not beat the shipped detector, while naming the real exposure (the first payments before the alarm) and the attack not tested (pieces to several different payees).

5. Limits

  • One attack shape (equal pieces, fixed gap, one payee). A scammer can vary amounts, gaps and payees; a guard keyed on one payee does nothing against pieces spread over several new payees (mule accounts).
  • The guard counts a retried payment twice if the retry comes after the 3-second de-duplication window; the false-alarm row above is the measured cost of that on the simulator, which may not reproduce real retry behaviour.
  • SatarkSim has no real splitting behaviour in it, so the unattacked numbers are the simulator's, not a measure of how often real scams split.
